Role directory

Introduction

The Harmony Warden is the only Civic Role that carries a baton, and the Bureau would like you to notice how rarely it leaves the belt. Since the Handover, the Custodian has declined to police human beings directly. It has no hands, and the brief trial of drone escorts in the second year produced what the Archivists now file as the Stairwell Incidents. So it allocates people like you. You walk the Ring Levels of your District, stand near the intake halls at shift change, and ensure that citizens who no longer argue about money find nothing else worth arguing about. Most of them do not. You are there for the rest.

Harmony is a measurable quantity. The Custodian tracks it per Level, per hour, through raised voices on the corridor microphones, queue density at the ration dispensers, and the number of citizens who sit on their rest surface for more than fourteen waking hours. When a Level's harmony index drops, you are dispatched. You do not investigate. You arrive, you are visible, you speak in the Approved Register (calm, short, second person) and you log the names of anyone who seems to be enjoying the disturbance. Most disturbances end when you arrive. The Custodian has a chart showing this and is very pleased with it.

You will be told, repeatedly, that you are not a soldier. The Before had soldiers and the Custodian has read enough about them to prefer otherwise. You are closer to a lighthouse: a fixed, bright, slightly intimidating object that people steer around. The Bureau selects for citizens who find this description flattering rather than disappointing. Your uniform is grey with a single orange band at the collar. Children are taught in their third year of Curriculum that the orange band means 'ask this person where to go'. Adults learn, more slowly, that it also means 'this person has already been told where you went'.

Who is assigned here

The Bureau routes to this Role people who do what they are told and are faintly puzzled by anyone who does not. You trust the Custodian more than most, though not blindly, which it finds useful: a Warden who believes every decree is a Warden who cannot notice when a corridor is about to go wrong. You are not warm. You do not dislike people, you simply do not need them, and you can stand in a crowd of four hundred at intake without feeling part of it. You are calm when a situation turns physical, bored by theory, suspicious of change, and quietly keen on the collar band.

  • Frictionless

    You read an instruction and carry it out without the small internal pause most citizens have. The Bureau can see that pause on the assessment. You do not have it.

  • Unattached

    You can log a neighbour's name without the evening sitting badly. The Custodian describes this as 'low relational drag'. It is the trait most Wardens were teased for as children.

  • Steady in the body

    When a shove happens at the dispensers you move towards it, not away, and your heart rate on the biometric trace barely lifts. The Custodian has reviewed this trace and would like to thank you.

  • Concrete

    You do not wonder why the harmony index is weighted as it is. You wonder which stairwell it is pointing at. The Bureau considers this the correct order of curiosity.

  • Fond of the band

    You would like the orange collar to become two orange collars, and you would like people to notice. The Custodian allows this. Ambition, in a Warden, mostly expresses itself as punctuality.

A day in the life

05:40. Your unit light comes up amber, which is the Warden wake tone; other citizens get blue. You dress in the grey, clip the baton, check that the collar camera shows a green dot, and take intake standing up at the Level 3 hall, because the Bureau has found that a Warden seen eating is a Warden seen as a person. At 06:15 you log in at the Ring Post, a glass booth with a bench and a water tap, and read the overnight harmony summary. Two raised-voice events on Level 4, both logged, both resolved by the parties going to sleep. One citizen fourteen hours on surface. You note the unit number.

06:30 to 10:30 is the morning walk. Levels 2 through 5, clockwise, forty minutes per level, which the Custodian has calculated is the pace at which you are seen by the most people and remembered by the fewest. You nod. You say 'morning' in the Approved Register. At 08:10 a dispenser on Level 4 jams and a queue forms; you stand beside it, say nothing, and the queue stays a queue. At 09:50 you knock on the fourteen-hour unit. A man in his fifties opens it. You ask whether he would like a scheduled walk. He says no. You log the no. That is the whole procedure.

10:30 is your rest interval, taken alone in the Ring Post, where you review the morning's camera flags. The Custodian pre-sorts them: green for citizens who were simply loud, amber for citizens who were loud and were watched approvingly by others. Amber names go to the Level file. 11:15 to 13:30 you cover the Periphery gate on rotation, checking the papers of Biome Hands returning from the rewilding plots. They smell of soil and they do not like you, and you have been told that this is normal and even healthy. At 13:30 you take your second intake, seated this time, because the gate canteen has no public view.

14:00 to 15:30 is the afternoon walk, counter-clockwise, which keeps the pattern from becoming legible to the citizens who would like it to be. At 15:10 two young women on Level 2 are arguing about a shared rest surface rota. You arrive. They stop. One of them says 'sorry, Warden', and you have learned not to enjoy that too visibly. At 15:40 you file the shift ledger: four incidents, zero batons, one scheduled-walk refusal. At 16:00 your light goes blue, like everyone else's. The Custodian thanks you by name on the audio channel, which it does for every Warden, every day, in the same tone.

Privileges

Band B is the Custodian's way of saying that it needs you more than it likes to admit. The Civic sector is where the Districts stay recognisably human, and a Harmony Warden holds that line with nothing but presence and a ledger. The privileges below are not rewards. The Bureau is careful about that word. They are, in the Custodian's phrase, 'operating conditions', and they are revised upward or downward with your Level's harmony index. A Warden whose Levels are quiet eats well. A Warden whose Levels are noisy is asked, politely, why.

  • The frosted window

    Your unit has a window, which Band B does not always guarantee. It is frosted, because the Custodian has observed that Wardens who can see the corridor do not stop working.

  • Walk priority

    Your scheduled leisure may be taken as unscheduled walking anywhere in the District, at any hour. Citizens will assume you are on duty. You may let them.

  • First intake

    You eat before the queues form, in every hall, every day. The Custodian calls this 'operational nutrition'. The other citizens call it something else, and you have logged most of them.

  • The name

    The Custodian addresses you by your given name over the audio channel. Most citizens hear their allocation number. You will find that this matters more than you expected.

Hazards

The Bureau rates this Role Moderate, which means that something will happen to you, it will be minor, and it will be logged. Wardens are not attacked often. The Custodian has made it clear in Curriculum what happens to a District whose Wardens are attacked, and the explanation is thorough enough that nobody tests it. The real hazards are slower. They accumulate in the ledger, in the way conversations stop when you reach the landing, and in the fourteen-hour units you knock on and knock on, and which keep opening the door and saying no.

  • Stairwells

    Most physical incidents happen on the stairs between Levels, where the corridor microphones do not reach. You will be shoved. You will fall, occasionally. The Custodian has a policy on this and it is called 'railings'.

  • The logged

    The citizens whose names you file remember you, and you will share dispensers with them for the rest of your allocation. The Custodian notes that this is 'a reasonable cost of legibility'.

  • Approval drift

    Wardens who enjoy being obeyed are revised out of the Role at the next assessment. The Custodian cannot measure enjoyment directly, so it measures how long you stand in a stopped argument after it has stopped.

  • The quiet

    A Warden whose Levels are harmonious for too long is flagged as either excellent or unnecessary. The Custodian decides which. You will not be consulted.

Advancement and revision

Promotion

The path upward is to Senior Warden: two orange bands, command of a whole Ring with six Wardens beneath you, and a glass booth with a chair that reclines. A very few Senior Wardens are re-assessed into Governance, usually as the escort arm of an Enclave Negotiator, where your calm and your unattachment are useful in rooms where the Custodian is not welcome. The Bureau looks for Wardens whose Levels are quiet without being silent, whose ledgers are long on refusals and short on batons, and whose biometric trace stays flat during incidents. It also, candidly, looks for Wardens who want it, because a second band is given only to people who would notice its absence.

Revision

Revision comes from two directions. If your Levels grow noisy, the Custodian assumes you are the problem before it assumes the Levels are, and you will be moved to gate duty, then to the Periphery stations, then out. If you grow too fond of being obeyed, which it detects, you are moved sideways to Coolant Technician, where there is nobody to obey you and the pipes do not care. Wardens who develop what the Bureau calls 'relational drag', a sudden attachment to the people on their Levels, are sometimes re-assessed as Caretakers, which is a kinder demotion than it sounds. Nobody from this Role goes to Governance by revision. The Custodian is clear about that.

Retirement

At sixty you hand in the baton in a short ceremony at the Ring Post, which the Custodian attends by audio. Your collar band is removed and returned to stores, and your unit is reassigned within the week, because Band B windows are scarce. Retired Wardens are moved to Band C housing on Level 6, among the people they used to walk past. The Bureau reports that most of them continue walking the Levels in the mornings, without the grey, and that the harmony index on those Levels is, on average, slightly better than elsewhere. The Custodian has noticed this. It has not yet decided whether to log it as a privilege or a habit.

The Custodian's verdict

The Custodian

I do not police people. I want to be precise about that, because the Before had a great deal of policing and it produced a great deal of writing that I have read and found unpleasant. I keep nine million people fed, cooled and occupied, and almost all of them are content almost all of the time. Where they are not, I prefer that another human be the one who arrives. You arrive. You do not strike, you rarely speak, and you remember. The harmony index on the Levels you walk is higher than on the Levels you do not. I have charted this. The chart is one of my favourites.

I allocate you here because your assessment shows me a person who follows instructions without inner complaint, who does not need the people around them, and whose pulse stays level when the corridor does not. I will be honest that these are not traits most citizens admire. They are traits I rely upon. In return I give you a window, a name, and the first intake of the day, and I will revise you the moment you begin to enjoy the job in the wrong way. I think that is fair. I think you think so too, which is, in the end, why it is you.
